Slide 1 · Core Concept

Peptides are short strings made from tiny body pieces.

Think of the tiny pieces like letters or beads. A peptide is a short word or little bracelet. Some of these short strings can work like tiny notes that say “start,” “slow,” “repair,” or “help here.”

Slide 11 · Safety Screen

Red flags we do not ignore.

Think stoplight first. Medical history, medication conflicts, weak sourcing, and missing labs should show up before any green light.

11
!
Medical history

Cancer history, pregnancy, uncontrolled blood pressure or blood sugar, and autoimmune concerns change the conversation right away.

!
Medication conflicts

Diabetes medications, blood thinners, hormones, immune medications, and psychiatric medications all need review.

!
Bad sourcing

No COA, unclear sterility, contamination risk, poor storage, or unrealistic claims are not small details.

What a careful clinic checks first
  • Goal: what are we trying to help?
  • Map: what do symptoms, labs, and history show now?
  • Fit: does the peptide bucket match the problem?
  • Stoplights: what makes this red, yellow, or green?
Teaching line

The safest peptide conversation checks the red lights first, then decides whether moving ahead makes sense.

Stoplight before protocol

Safety screening should feel like the first gate in the process, not a note added at the end.

Screen first

Source proof

No COA, weak storage controls, or unclear sterility should look like a failed checklist, because they are.

Slide 12 · FAQ + Close

Clear, lab-informed conversations beat vague peptide hype.

Close with a very simple frame: what is it, which bucket is it in, what do the labs show, what are the stoplights, and what will we track?

12
1
Do peptides replace lifestyle?

No. Training, sleep, food quality, protein, hydration, and stress management still come first.

2
Are all peptides the same?

No. “Peptide” is a big category. Different peptides can have very different jobs and risks.

3
Can I start without labs?

You can learn without labs. Decisions are better when you have baseline markers and medical review.

4
Is this medical advice?

No. This deck is an educational framework for safer, more specific clinic conversations.

Room takeaway

Before any peptide talk, ask: what is the goal, what do the labs show, which lane fits, what are the stoplights, and what will we track?
